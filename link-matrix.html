How the maths works. Data rate = pixel clock × bits per pixel. Pixel clock uses CVT-RBv2 timing, 80 px of horizontal blanking and a 460 µs vertical blank, which is what a modern display or processor will ask for. Bits per pixel is 3 × depth for RGB, 2 × for 4:2:2, 1.5 × for 4:2:0. The link's raw rate is cut by its line coding, 8b/10b for HDMI up to 2.0 and DisplayPort up to 1.4 (80%), 16b/18b for HDMI 2.1 FRL (89%), 128b/132b for DisplayPort 2.x UHBR (97%). Real processors and EDIDs vary, so treat a fit under 10% headroom as marginal and try a lower refresh or 4:2:2 before show day. Thunderbolt carries DisplayPort inside it, so TB3 and TB4 behave as DP 1.4 and TB5 as DP 2.1. On Apple silicon, DSC is on whenever a 6K or 8K panel asks for it, but a video processor has to support it too, and many don't.
